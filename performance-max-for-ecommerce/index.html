Performance Max decides nearly everything.
You control the inputs.

It takes a budget and a goal and allocates on its own across Search, Shopping, YouTube, Display, Gmail and Maps. That makes the handful of things you do control — the feed, the values, the exclusions and what you let it bid on — the entire job.

E-commerce & DTC

Performance Max is the campaign type most e-commerce accounts now run most of their budget through, and the one people can say least about. It reports well almost by default, which is the problem: a campaign that is allowed to bid on your own brand name will show a return that looks like competence and is mostly arithmetic. Everything that actually decides whether it works happens upstream of the campaign.

What you actually control

You can't choose placements, you can't see most of the search terms, and you can't bid by keyword. What's left is a short list, and it is the whole job:

  • The product feed. There are no keywords here. Google reads the product data to decide which searches you appear for, which makes the feed your targeting.
  • Conversion values. What you tell it an order is worth is what it optimizes toward. This is the steering wheel and most accounts leave it set to revenue.
  • What it's allowed to bid on. Brand exclusions and the account-level negatives that decide whether the campaign is buying demand or harvesting it.
  • How the catalog is divided. One campaign over a mixed-margin catalog is one lever for products that deserve different targets.
  • The assets. Whether you hand it a feed alone or a feed plus images and video materially changes where the money goes.
  • Audience signals. A starting hint, not a targeting setting, and worth treating as the former.

Six inputs. Everything else is Google's.

It will take credit for your brand searches

This is the first thing to check in any Performance Max account and it is wrong more often than it's right.

People searching your brand name were going to buy. They are the cheapest conversions in the account. Performance Max is eligible to serve on those searches, and when it does, their revenue lands in the campaign's return. The number goes up, the campaign looks like your best performer, and you have learned nothing about whether it can find customers who weren't already looking for you.

The fix is to make the campaign prove it on cold demand: brand handled deliberately in its own place, brand excluded from Performance Max, and the question reframed from what the campaign's ROAS is to what it added. If total revenue doesn't move when the campaign's reported revenue does, it was rearranging credit rather than producing sales.

Conversion value is the steering wheel

Performance Max allocates toward whatever you have told it is valuable. If every order reports the same way, it will reliably find the orders that are easiest to get, which in most catalogs means the cheapest products at the thinnest margins.

So the values have to carry something real. Passing contribution margin rather than revenue changes what the campaign chases, and it is usually the single highest-impact change available in an e-commerce account. The reasoning behind that sits on the e-commerce PPC page, and it applies to Performance Max more sharply than anywhere else, because here you can't correct a bad target by hand afterward.

The new customer acquisition setting is a related lever. It lets you tell the campaign that a first-time buyer is worth more than a repeat one, which is often true and almost never reflected in the default setup. It's worth using when you can say what a new customer is actually worth over time, and worth leaving alone when you can't, because the number you invent becomes the number it optimizes toward.

You can't negative your way out of trouble

In Search, a bad search term is a negative keyword and the problem is over. Performance Max gives you category-level reporting rather than the full term report, and the controls are blunter — account-level negatives and campaign exclusions rather than surgical ones.

The practical consequence is that prevention moves upstream. Problems that would be a quick negative in a Search campaign have to be solved in the feed instead: product titles that invite the wrong searches, product types and categories that place an item in the wrong neighbourhood, items that shouldn't be advertised at all left in the feed because nobody excluded them. A clean feed is worth more here than in any other campaign type, and a messy one is harder to recover from.

One campaign over a mixed catalog is one lever

Handing the whole catalog to a single campaign asks one target to serve products with different margins, different price points and different jobs. It will favour whatever converts most easily, which is rational and frequently the opposite of what the business needs.

Dividing the catalog by what a product is worth to you rather than by what aisle it sits in is almost always the better structure: a tier for the products that carry the margin, a tier for the ones that bring new customers in, a tier for the long tail that should run on a lower target or not at all. Platform specifics for Shopify catalogs — variants, item grouping, custom labels built from cost — are on the Shopify page.

Feed-only or full assets is a decision, not a default

An asset group with nothing but the feed behaves much like Shopping. Add images, headlines and video and the campaign becomes eligible for YouTube, Display and Gmail, and a meaningful share of the budget will go there.

Neither is wrong. What's wrong is arriving at one by accident. If you have no video, Google will generate one, and an auto-assembled video running against your brand is a decision somebody should have made on purpose. Thin assets are a genuine performance problem rather than a cosmetic one, so the honest options are to resource the assets properly or to stay close to feed-only and accept the narrower reach.

When standard Shopping is still the right answer

Performance Max is not an upgrade, it's a trade: more inventory and less control. Sometimes that trade is bad.

A small catalog where every product matters is easier to run with explicit control. An account being actively diagnosed benefits from being able to see what's happening. A store with a few products that must be defended specifically, or one whose feed isn't clean yet, will get more from standard Shopping until the inputs are right — because Performance Max rewards good inputs and conceals bad ones, which is exactly the wrong property while you're still finding the problems.

We'd rather tell you to wait on it than move your budget into a campaign type that will hide why the account isn't working.

How an engagement runs

Month to month, with no long-term contract. The ad account, the Merchant Center and the conversion tracking are yours and stay yours. Reporting is in revenue and margin rather than in campaign ROAS, for the reason the whole page is about: the campaign's own number is the one it is least safe to trust.

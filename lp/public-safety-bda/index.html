Start here

It starts with an RF grid test

We split the building into a grid and measure every point on the public safety frequencies. Any point under the minimum fails. You get a report written for your fire official.

If it passes

You get the documentation and help closing it out. No system, no equipment, nothing sold.

If it fails

The same data becomes the design: which squares failed, by how much, and what the donor signal gives us to work with.

  • Final inspection coming and nobody has tested radio coverage.
  • The building failed its radio test, or the submittal came back rejected.
  • An existing BDA is faulting, oscillating or dead on the panel.
  • Annual testing is overdue, or you are budgeting a build.

A failed radio test can hold a finished building empty.

Which is why we test before your final inspection is on the calendar — not after.

BDA and ERRC, answered

A grid test answers it. We measure signal strength and audio quality at every point on the local public safety frequencies. Pass, and you submit the report and install nothing. Fail, and those measurements become the design basis.

Typically −95 dBm inbound at DAQ 3.0 or better, across 95% of general areas and 99% of critical areas — fire command centers, exit stairs, elevator lobbies and fire pump rooms.

It depends on square footage, floors, construction type and donor signal strength. Anyone quoting firm before testing is guessing. We price it once the grid test says what the building needs — and if it passes, nothing.

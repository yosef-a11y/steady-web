Where Meta fits

Google captures demand. Meta creates it.

Someone searching on Google already wants what you sell. Someone scrolling Instagram doesn't know yet. That one difference changes how every part of a Meta account should be built.

1. Reach people before they search

Meta finds buyers who match your best customers before they ever type a query. For services people don't search for, or products they don't know exist yet, it is often the only channel with real volume.

2. Let creative do the targeting

With broad audiences and Advantage+ placements, the ad itself decides who responds. Testing hooks, offers and formats does more for cost per lead than any interest stack.

3. Feed it real outcomes

The algorithm optimizes toward whatever you tell it is a conversion. Send it booked calls, qualified leads and purchases through the Conversions API, not just raw form submits.

Why most Meta accounts stall

The problem is almost never the targeting

Most businesses that come to us about Facebook ads have already tried them. The pattern is familiar: the first few weeks look promising, cost per lead drops, the dashboard fills up with form submissions, and then someone on the sales side points out that half the numbers are wrong, nobody answers, and the ones who do answer don't remember filling anything in.

That isn't bad luck. It's what happens when a Meta campaign is told to optimize for the cheapest possible lead. The algorithm is very good at its job. If the job is “get form fills,” it finds the people most likely to tap a pre-filled form by accident, and it gets better at finding them every week.

The fix is to change what the campaign is being asked to do. That means tracking what happens after the lead arrives, sending that information back to Meta, and building the offer and the form so that the people who finish it are people who meant to.

What we build in a Meta Ads account

  • Tracking that survives privacy changes. The Meta Pixel on its own misses a meaningful share of conversions since iOS privacy changes. We set up the Conversions API alongside it, so purchases and leads are reported server-side and the algorithm has enough signal to learn from.
  • Lead quality signals. For lead generation, we pass back which leads became qualified, booked or closed, so Meta can optimize toward the people who turn into customers rather than the people who fill in forms.
  • A creative testing system. Meta rewards fresh, specific creative. We test angles, hooks and formats on a schedule, retire what fatigues, and keep a record of what each test taught us so the next round starts smarter.
  • Simple campaign structure. Fewer campaigns with more budget each give the algorithm enough data to exit the learning phase. Splitting a modest budget across a dozen ad sets is one of the most common reasons accounts never stabilize.
  • Landing pages or instant forms, chosen on purpose. Instant forms are cheap and fast but often produce weaker leads. A landing page adds friction that filters. We pick based on your sales process, and test both when the answer isn't obvious.

Meta Ads for lead generation

Home services, healthcare, professional services and local businesses can get strong lead flow from Facebook and Instagram, especially where search volume for the service is thin. Home care is a good example: families rarely search for it until they need it, but the people who will need it soon are very reachable on Meta.

The work that matters most is upstream of the ad. A clear offer, a form that asks the questions your intake team needs answered, and a follow-up process fast enough that a lead is contacted while they still remember the ad. Speed to lead affects results on Meta more than almost any setting inside Ads Manager.

Meta Ads for e-commerce

For online stores, Meta is where new customers discover a brand. Advantage+ shopping campaigns, catalog ads and retargeting all play a role, but they only pay when the numbers underneath are right: product margin, return rates, and what a first order is really worth once repeat purchases are counted.

We report Meta's contribution alongside Google Shopping and Performance Max, because the two channels overlap. A customer who saw your Instagram ad and later searched your brand name on Google will often be counted by both platforms. Reporting each in isolation flatters both of them.

Special ad categories, honestly

If you advertise housing, employment, credit or financial products, or certain social and political topics, Meta restricts your targeting options. Age, ZIP code and detailed interest targeting are limited. That doesn't make Meta useless for these businesses, but it changes the plan, and we'd rather tell you that before we start than discover it after the first rejected ad.

Recruiting is a common example. Hiring campaigns run under the employment category, which is one reason recruiting ads for BCBAs and RBTs lean on Google Search as well as Meta.

When we'll tell you to skip Meta

Meta isn't the answer for every business. If people already search for what you sell, in volume, Google usually turns budget into customers faster. If your average sale is small and your margins are thin, the testing cost of Meta can outrun the return. And if nobody is available to call leads back quickly, the leads will go cold no matter how good the ads are. We'll say so on the audit call.

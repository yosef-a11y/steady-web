Addiction treatment & recovery

Treatment is one of the most expensive categories in all of paid search, and the reason is simple arithmetic: an admission is worth a great deal, so a lot of well-funded organizations are willing to pay a lot for the click that might produce one. That does not make the category a bad bet. It makes it a category where a half-funded campaign does not return half the admissions. It returns almost none, because you are outbid on every search that was going to matter and left with the ones nobody else wanted.

Start with what an admission is worth to you

Before anything is built, two numbers decide whether this channel is worth running at all: what a completed admission is worth to your program, and how many inquiries your intake team converts into one.

Those two numbers set the ceiling on what you can afford to pay for an inquiry, and therefore whether the category's cost per click is survivable for you. Programs with detox usually find the math works, because the economics of that level of care can carry an acquisition cost that outpatient alone often cannot. Programs without it, running a single facility against national advertisers, frequently find it doesn't — and it's better to know that in week one.

We'd rather do that arithmetic with you up front and tell you the channel isn't worth funding than take a budget and report impressions for six months.

You need to be approved to advertise before any of this starts

Google restricts who may advertise addiction services, and advertisers have to be certified before their ads are allowed to run. That approval is the operator's to obtain and it has to be in place before a campaign can launch. It isn't something an agency does on your behalf, and any plan that skips it isn't a plan.

We work with programs that arrive already approved. If you aren't yet, get that sorted first; there is nothing for us to build until it's done.

Most of the work is admissions, not advertising

This is the part that decides outcomes, and it is almost entirely inside your building rather than inside the ad account.

Speed to answer. A family or a prospective client in this category is calling more than one place, often in a narrow window where they've decided to act. A call that goes to voicemail is usually a call that goes to whoever picks up next. If the phone isn't answered live, extending hours is a better investment than raising bids.

Verification of benefits. How quickly your team can verify coverage and give a straight answer about what a stay will cost determines how many verified inquiries turn into admissions. Where that process is slow or inconsistent, more spend simply produces a longer queue of people who drift away while waiting.

Census. Advertising should be spending against the beds you actually have. A campaign running flat while census swings produces waste in one direction and missed admissions in the other. Budget that moves with available capacity is worth more than a cleverer bid strategy.

What gets tracked, and what we won't report as a win

A form fill is not an admission. Neither is a phone call, and neither is a verified benefit. Each of those is a step, and a campaign can produce a great many of the early ones while producing none of the last one.

So the account gets wired to the real stages: inquiry, contact made, benefits verified, admitted. Where your CRM can send those stages back, the bidding optimizes toward the one that matters rather than the one that's easiest to count. Where it can't, we build the reporting so you can at least see where the drop happens, and we say plainly which number we're optimizing against in the meantime.

You'll also be told which searches are producing nothing. In this category a large share of spend goes to queries that look relevant and convert at zero — people researching for a paper, job-seekers, people looking for free or state-funded options when you aren't one, and people in other states entirely. Cutting those is unglamorous and it's usually where the first real gain comes from.

What we don't do

We don't buy leads from third parties and resell them to you, and we don't put your campaigns on a call-tracking or CRM stack that belongs to us. You own the ad account, the data and the tracking. If you leave, all of it stays with you and keeps working.

We also don't guarantee an admission count. Anyone who does is either pricing that risk into the retainer or defining the win loosely enough that it can't be missed. What we'll commit to is telling you what the category costs, what your numbers have to be for it to work, and when it isn't working.

Month to month, with no long-term contract. The account, the conversion tracking and the data are yours from the start and stay yours. Reporting is to the stage you care about rather than to a dashboard of impressions, and if the honest read in month three is that the category isn't returning what it costs you, you'll hear that from us rather than discover it yourself.

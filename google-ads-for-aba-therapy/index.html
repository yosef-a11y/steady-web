Why this is different

ABA is not ordinary lead generation

Most agencies run an ABA account the way they run a plumber: maximize form fills, report the cost per lead, call it a win. That misreads the business in three ways, and each one costs money.

1. The family is not price shopping

Services are typically funded through insurance or a state programme. The parent filling in your form is not comparing your rate to a competitor's. They are asking three questions: do you take my plan, do you have availability, and how soon can we be seen. An ad that leads with price answers none of them. An ad that names accepted plans and realistic timelines answers all three, and filters out the enquiries you cannot serve before they consume intake time.

2. Lifetime value is long, so the cost ceiling is high

An authorized client may receive services for months or years. Owners who anchor on a $60 cost per lead from another industry routinely underbid a channel that could profitably pay several times that. The number that matters is not cost per form fill. It is cost per authorized start, and the gap between the two is usually large: a meaningful share of enquiries fail insurance verification, never schedule an evaluation, or do not complete one.

Until you measure that funnel, you are bidding blind. Which brings up the thing almost nobody has set up properly.

3. Optimize to the deep event, not the form

Google's bidding will get you more of whatever you tell it to value. Tell it to value form submissions, and it will find you people who like filling in forms. The fix is to feed the later stages back:

  1. Form submitted — the only event most accounts track.
  2. Insurance verified — the first real qualifier.
  3. Evaluation scheduled, then completed.
  4. Authorization received and services started — the event that actually pays for staff.

Pushing those back into Google Ads as offline conversions, with values attached, changes what the algorithm optimizes toward. It is the single highest-leverage change in most ABA accounts, and it is unglamorous plumbing work rather than a creative idea.

The campaign most providers are missing: hiring

Ask an ABA owner what limits growth and the answer is rarely demand. It is BCBAs and RBTs. Yet the paid budget almost always goes entirely to client acquisition, while recruitment is left to job boards.

Recruitment campaigns are a different build: different keywords, different landing pages, different conversion. They also tend to be cheaper per hire than agency placement fees, and the arithmetic is easy to check against what you currently pay. If your waitlist is long because you cannot staff it, more client leads make the problem worse, not better.

What Google's health rules actually restrict

This is where well-meaning agencies create risk. Google restricts personalized advertising based on health conditions, and that has practical consequences:

  • No remarketing audiences built on health status. You cannot build a list of "people who visited the autism services page" and target it as such.
  • Be careful what the tracking sends. Conversion data, form fields and page paths should not carry diagnostic or personally identifying health information. Audit what your tag is actually transmitting.
  • Watch the ad copy. Language that implies knowledge of a user's condition can get disapproved, and in some cases should be.

None of this stops you advertising. It shapes how the account is built, and it is worth getting right the first time rather than after an audit.

Where the money usually leaks

  • Broad match without negatives. "ABA therapy" collects students researching coursework, people looking for degree programmes, and job seekers. All of them click. None of them enroll.
  • Targeting a radius you cannot staff. Clicks from areas with no clinician coverage convert into enquiries you have to turn down.
  • Insurance mismatch. If you do not take a plan, say so before the click, not after an intake call.
  • Slow follow-up. In intake-driven businesses, response time dominates almost every other variable. The best campaign in the world cannot outrun a two-day callback.

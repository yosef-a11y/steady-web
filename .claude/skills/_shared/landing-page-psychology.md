# Landing Page Psychology: 9 Conversion Principles

Reference for the `steady` and `errc` skills. Use it when writing, auditing or comparing landing pages.
Source: summarized from Alisha Conlin-Hurd, "9 Landing Page Cheat Codes to DOUBLE Your Leads" (YouTube).

## The 9 principles

| # | Principle | Do | Avoid |
|---|-----------|----|-------|
| 1 | Affective priming | Hero image and headline set the right emotion (aspiration, relief, confidence) before reading starts. Use purposeful images that demonstrate the outcome. | Fear or "burnout" imagery unless the niche needs it. Tone that shifts (scary headline, cheery body). Decorative stock images. |
| 2 | Selective attention | Assume people scan. Pattern-interrupt, then point the attention at the conversion path. Remove distractions. | Attention-grabbers with no next step. Competing CTAs or links. |
| 3 | Proof stacking | Put proof after every major claim, all the way down the page. Repeat the core promise (truth effect). Show volume (consensus/social validation). Use native-looking proof such as screenshots, DMs and raw video. | Relying only on a testimonial carousel. Generic praise ("they're amazing"). Putting all the proof at the bottom. |
| 4 | Chunking | Subheads, lead bullets, numbered frameworks (e.g. "the 3-step X method"), white space, one idea per block. | Walls of text. Lists with no grouping. The whole offer in one block. Crammed layouts. |
| 5 | Authenticity | Raw, imperfect, real assets: phone video, simple statics, founder voice. | Staged or fake assets. Sterile corporate tone. Anything untrue. |
| 6 | Narrative transportation | Open with the reader's "before" state and pull them into the story, then bring in the offer as the bridge. Show the transformation. | Opening with the offer. Feature-feature-feature copy. Generic case studies with no before/after. |
| 7 | Curiosity gap | Headlines and section leads that create tension or mystery with a specific payoff ("even if you…"). | Big claims with no tension. Fake curiosity or clickbait the page never pays off. |
| 8 | Loss aversion | Show what waiting or doing nothing costs. Use real deadlines and real scarcity. Balance loss with the desired gain. | Fake urgency, scarcity where it makes no sense, fearmongering, loss-only framing (attracts poor-fit leads). |
| 9 | Authority bias | Put credentials high on the page. Borrow authority through client and media logos, expert or peer testimonials, and speaking or stage photos. | Invented credentials. Claims without proof. Authority buried at the bottom. Badge and logo overload. |

## Scoring rubric (for audits and competitor analysis)

Score each principle 0 to 2:

- **0:** absent or done wrong
- **1:** present but weak
- **2:** strong

Maximum total is 18. Any 0 is a priority fix.

## Recommended page order

1. Hero: priming, a curiosity-gap headline, a clear CTA, and authority above the fold
2. "Before" story: narrative transportation
3. Offer, chunked into a framework
4. Proof stacked after each claim
5. Loss and urgency framing (real only) next to the CTA
6. Final CTA with a proof recap

## Pre-ship checklist

- [ ] The page keeps one emotional tone from top to bottom
- [ ] Every attention element leads toward the CTA
- [ ] Every testimonial is specific (numbers, timeframe, outcome)
- [ ] Proof appears in at least 3 places
- [ ] No block runs longer than about 4 lines without a break, and every section has a subhead
- [ ] Authority is visible without scrolling
- [ ] All urgency, scarcity and credentials are true

## Guardrail

Never invent proof, urgency, scarcity or authority. If the inputs are missing, ask the client for them instead of fabricating.

# Industry page queue

One industry page per week. **Yosef's read is gathered before the page is written** — he
knows which categories are brutal, and a page that says so honestly converts better than
one that pretends otherwise. Never write an industry page without his notes below.

Status values: `approved` · `approved, with caveats` · `needs Yosef's read` · `declined`

## Approved — his notes shape the page

### Multifamily
**Status:** approved, with caveats
**Yosef:** "Doesn't really work unless there are a lot of searches for some identifier or
landmark nearby. For example people search for an apartment near Rodeo Drive or some other
attraction."

The whole page hangs on this qualifier and it's a genuinely useful one. Multifamily paid
search works when the property has a searchable anchor — a landmark, a campus, a district,
an employer — that people actually type. Without one, search volume is too thin and the
budget belongs elsewhere. Make that the organizing idea, not a footnote.

## Built

- **Legal & personal injury** — `/google-ads-for-law-firms/` (3 Nov 2026). Yosef's caveat is the
  headline, literally: the H1 is "the most expensive category in paid search" and the hero says
  that funded at half the going rate it produces almost none of the cases, not half of them. The
  page is built to filter — it tells a firm which practice areas are realistically fundable (estate
  planning, immigration, employment, family; not PI in a major metro on a small budget), sets the
  budget backwards from case value and lead-to-signed rate, and closes with a concrete "if the
  budget isn't there yet" section rather than leaving the warning hanging. Also covers Local
  Services Ads and their verification requirement, intake and after-hours coverage as the largest
  controllable variable, who else is in the auction (lead sellers monetize the same click more than
  once), and keeping ad copy reviewable against state bar rules without us interpreting them. No
  CPCs or figures invented; no legal clients implied.

- **Home care & senior care** — `/home-care-marketing/` (27 Oct 2026). Yosef's caveat is the
  organizing idea: whether Google works is a question about the territory, not the agency, so the
  page opens by telling the reader to measure query volume inside the actual service radius before
  funding search. Meta carries equal weight throughout rather than appearing as a footnote —
  it reaches the adult child before the search happens, which is most of the demand. Also covers
  who actually inquires (adult child, often out of state), the categories families confuse,
  payment questions including that Medicare doesn't cover custodial care the way they assume,
  speed to lead, tracking to start of care with authorized hours, and caregiver recruiting as a
  separate account. No figures invented; no clients implied.

- **Nonprofits & institutions** — `/google-ads-for-nonprofits/` (22 Oct 2026). Built around the
  Google Ad Grant: why the allowance is a ceiling rather than a target, the constraints a paid
  account doesn't have, why conversion tracking is the gate on everything, what the grant is
  genuinely good at versus what it's sold as, and how to handle a suspension. Thresholds
  described as mechanisms and the reader pointed at Google's own documentation, since the
  program's numbers move.

## Rules for these pages

Everything in `.claude/skills/_shared/landing-page-psychology.md` applies, plus:

- **His caveats are the selling point, not a disclaimer.** An agency that tells you a
  category is expensive before you hire them is more credible than one that doesn't. Put
  the warning in the body copy where a buyer will read it, not in fine print.
- **Never claim clients in an industry the reviews don't evidence.** The homepage reviews
  are the record. An industry page describes method and approach; it does not imply a
  roster.
- **Never invent figures.** No CPCs, no budgets, no conversion rates, no client counts.
  Where a number would help, describe the mechanism instead.
- Minimum 900 words of real prose, or pick something else.

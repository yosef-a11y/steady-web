# Industry page queue

One industry page per week. **Yosef's read is gathered before the page is written** — he
knows which categories are brutal, and a page that says so honestly converts better than
one that pretends otherwise. Never write an industry page without his notes below.

Status values: `approved` · `approved, with caveats` · `needs Yosef's read` · `declined`

---

## Approved — his notes shape the page

### Home care & senior care
**Status:** approved, with caveats
**Yosef:** "Good fit, but it depends on local search for Google to work. We can do great
work getting leads with Meta Ads."

The page must not sell Google Ads as the answer everywhere. Lead with the honest version:
Google works where there's enough local search volume for the service and the area, and
where there isn't, Meta is the stronger lead channel. This is the first page on the site
where Meta carries equal weight — don't bury it.

### Legal & personal injury
**Status:** approved, with caveats
**Yosef:** "Very expensive. Competitive. If willing to invest, can work well."

Say the budget reality plainly, early, above the fold if it fits. The page should filter
out firms who can't fund it rather than collect inquiries it can't serve.

### Multifamily
**Status:** approved, with caveats
**Yosef:** "Doesn't really work unless there are a lot of searches for some identifier or
landmark nearby. For example people search for an apartment near Rodeo Drive or some other
attraction."

The whole page hangs on this qualifier and it's a genuinely useful one. Multifamily paid
search works when the property has a searchable anchor — a landmark, a campus, a district,
an employer — that people actually type. Without one, search volume is too thin and the
budget belongs elsewhere. Make that the organizing idea, not a footnote.

## Rules for these pages

Everything in `.claude/skills/_shared/landing-page-psychology.md` applies, plus:

- **His caveats are the selling point, not a disclaimer.** An agency that tells you a
  category is expensive before you hire them is more credible than one that doesn't. Put
  the warning in the body copy where a buyer will read it, not in fine print.
- **Never claim clients in an industry the reviews don't evidence.** The homepage reviews
  are the record. An industry page describes method and approach; it does not imply a
  roster.
- **Never invent figures.** No CPCs, no budgets, no conversion rates, no client counts.
  Where a number would help, describe the mechanism instead.
- Minimum 900 words of real prose, or pick something else.

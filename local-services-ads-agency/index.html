How LSA is different

Not a search campaign with a badge on it

Local Services Ads run on their own system, with their own rules for who shows up. Most of the levers that matter aren't bids.

1. You pay per lead

A call, message or booking from a customer in your area and category is what you're charged for, not a click. Leads that don't fit can be disputed, and many businesses never do.

2. No keywords to manage

Google decides which searches trigger your listing based on your categories, job types and service area. Getting those settings exactly right does the work keyword lists do elsewhere.

3. Ranking is earned off the ad

Reviews, how quickly you answer, your business hours and your proximity all influence where you appear. Raising the budget doesn't buy the top spot on its own.

What we see in most accounts

Set up once, then left alone

Local Services Ads are often switched on by the business owner, or by a Google rep on the phone, and then left to run. The verification gets done, a weekly budget gets set, the leads start coming in, and nobody looks again until the bill seems too high or the phone goes quiet.

Left alone, an LSA account drifts. Job types that don't pay well keep generating leads. Service areas sprawl into towns you don't want to drive to. Leads that were wrong numbers, spam or the wrong service sit there undisputed and billed. Hours in the profile don't match when someone actually answers, so calls go to voicemail and the ranking slides.

None of these show up as an error. They show up as a cost per booked job that slowly gets worse. Managing LSA is mostly about catching these things every week.

What Local Services Ads management covers

  • Verification and setup. Background checks, license and insurance verification, and the business details Google needs for the Google Guaranteed or Google Screened badge. We keep the paperwork moving so the listing goes live without weeks of back-and-forth.
  • Job types and service areas. Turning on the jobs you want and off the ones you don't, and drawing service areas around where you can actually profit from the drive. This is the targeting in LSA, so it gets reviewed regularly.
  • Lead review and disputes. Every lead is reviewed. Ones that don't qualify under Google's rules, such as spam, wrong service or out-of-area calls, get disputed so you aren't paying for them.
  • Responsiveness and hours. Google favors businesses that answer. We line up your listed hours with when calls are really picked up, and set up message and booking handling so leads don't wait.
  • Reviews. Review count and rating weigh heavily in LSA ranking. We set up a simple, compliant process to ask happy customers for reviews, which also helps your Google Business Profile.
  • Budget and bidding. Choosing between maximizing leads and setting a target cost per lead, and adjusting the weekly budget to match how many jobs you can take on.

LSA and Google Search work better together

Local Services Ads and regular Google Search ads show up on the same results page. When they're run by different people, they compete with each other and nobody can say which one produced a job. When they're run together, LSA can cover the core high-intent searches it handles well, and Search can pick up the jobs, towns and queries LSA doesn't reach.

We also track both to the same outcome. A lead from LSA and a lead from a Search ad should be judged by how many of each turned into a booked job, not by which dashboard reports the lower cost per lead.

Who Local Services Ads are for

LSA is available for a defined list of categories, mostly home services such as HVAC, plumbing, roofing, electrical, cleaning, pest control and remodeling, plus some professional services such as legal, financial and real estate. The list and the rules differ by country and change over time, so the first step is confirming your category qualifies and what verification it requires.

It works best for businesses that can answer the phone during the hours they list, that serve a clear local area, and that can collect reviews steadily. If calls regularly go unanswered, fixing that will do more for your LSA results than anything we change in the account.

Serving New Jersey and beyond

We're based in Lakewood, in Ocean County, so we know the service-area problems specific to New Jersey: towns separated by a bridge you don't cross, shore demand that changes with the season, and storm-driven spikes for roofing and restoration. We also manage LSA for businesses elsewhere in the country. More on that on our New Jersey Google Ads page.

Straight answers

Local Services Ads, questions answered

Local Services Ads are a Google ad format for local service businesses that appears at the very top of the results page, usually with a Google Guaranteed or Google Screened badge. You pay per lead, such as a call, message or booking, rather than per click. Google decides when to show your listing based on your business category, job types, service area, reviews and responsiveness, not on keywords.

Your business goes through Google's verification for its category, which typically includes background checks, license verification and proof of insurance, along with a review of your business details. Requirements vary by category and location. We handle the setup and keep the process moving, but Google makes the final decision.

You can be, which is why reviewing leads matters. Google allows you to dispute leads that don't qualify under its rules, for example spam, wrong service, or a job outside your service area. Disputes have to be submitted within Google's time window, so we review leads regularly rather than at the end of the month.

Usually both, coordinated. LSA tends to deliver efficient leads for core high-intent searches in eligible categories. Search ads reach queries, services and areas LSA doesn't cover, and give you more control over messaging. Run together and tracked to booked jobs, they cover more of the page without competing against each other.

Google weighs your proximity to the customer, your review count and rating, how quickly you respond to calls and messages, your business hours, and your budget and bidding settings. More budget alone won't guarantee the top spot. Answering promptly during listed hours and building reviews steadily usually does more.
